# Gift shortlist and maker brief

Lead with **Ready to consider**, **Concept pending confirmation**, or **No good
fit**, and the reason. Only the first can have a top recommendation; a concept
is not a recommendation that passed sourcing. State buyer context and assumptions.

For each of at most three distinct ideas:

- Item and why it may be useful; score personal meaning, usefulness, originality,
  respect and feasibility 0–2 with a sentence of evidence per score. Missing
  admissible evidence, or a zero for respect or feasibility, vetoes recommendation
  regardless of total.
- Verified premise: fact ID, attributed creator, exact supporting excerpt,
  dated source link and checked-on date. List any supplemental public evidence
  separately. Name the creative inference, never present it as the creator's wish.
- Maker brief: material, dimensions/variant, exact text, artwork description and
  placement, quantity, proof requirement, supplied assets and rights to confirm.
  Do not reproduce protected character art or logos without permission.
- Where to order: exact item URL and supplier, destination country evidence,
  quantity-one/customization evidence, stock/availability check, price/currency,
  extras, production range, transit range, earliest/latest arrival estimate,
  buffer, customs/tax, gift packaging and source links for each material claim.
- Buyer confirmations: ownership/preferences, permissions for art, final proof,
  landed quote, address obtained from recipient/manager, PO box compatibility,
  destination and deadline. Do not make a hard budget/deadline promise while its
  required inputs remain unknown.

Compare with generic swag in one line: explain what personal fact improves this
gift, or admit there is no demonstrated advantage. State research limits, cost
and time separately from gift prices. Never make a rejected-detail appendix.

## Synthetic examples (not real creators or verified supplier offers)

**Rich profile:** a creator repeatedly says they annotate walking routes in a
paper notebook. A durable pocket notebook with an original trail-line motif
could be useful; the wish for a replacement is an inference. An engraved desk
object is a different alternative only if another fact supports its use. Without
verified stock, destination shipping and landed cost, both remain concepts.

**Thin profile:** one video reviews a coffee machine. No standing personal
preference and no admissible PROFILE facts: no good fit, rather than another mug.

**Changed profile:** an old hobby is marked ended, or recent disclosures conflict.
Do not print the hobby on an object, even if the supplier can deliver tomorrow.

**Multi-host:** a cohost's preference cannot personalize the primary host's gift.
Use a shared fact only for an explicitly shared gift; otherwise ask who receives it.

**Unavailable item or impossible deadline:** find a feasible local equivalent
with the same meaning, or return no feasible fit. A link alone does not rescue it.

## Later outreach comparison (design only)

Propose brand-funded, brand-sent gifts; confirm the budget owner and sender before
running. Randomly assign comparable eligible creators to personal gift plus
ordinary outreach versus the same outreach alone, stratifying relationship,
channel size, category and country. Keep sender, copy, offer and send day similar.
Document opt-outs, delivery failures and missing addresses before reading results.
Treat recipient/address consent and accepting a gift as separate from agreeing
to post or work with the brand. Do not require a post in exchange for a gift.

Predefine a 14-day qualified-reply window and 30-day booking window from outreach.
Measure by original assignment, count undelivered gifts, and report uncertainty
and group sizes. Track address-request attrition and delivery lag separately.
Record research/labor, item, customization, packing, shipping, customs and taxes;
compare incremental cost per qualified response and booking. Track voluntary
creator posts separately with dates and links; they are not guaranteed deliverables.
Three illustrative gifts establish neither demand nor causal lift. Decide sample
size using baseline response rate and a meaningful lift before starting.
